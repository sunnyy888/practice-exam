Part 1 · A/B testing (Q1–Q6)

Q1. Test design (3 pts)
The two pages see different visitors (weekday vs weekend), so the day mix is confounded with the page. If weekend shoppers convert better, B looks better than it is. Fix: randomize each visitor to A or B every day (e.g. 50/50 by a hash of the visitor ID), and keep returning visitors on their version.
  • 1 pt: confounding / the groups differ
  • 1 pt: correct direction (B inflated)
  • 1 pt: per-visitor randomization on every day
Q2. Two-proportion z-test (7 pts)
p_A = 0.0600, p_B = 0.0653, lift = 0.0053.
Pooled p̂ = (2,400 + 2,610) / 80,000 = 0.062625.
SE = √(p̂(1−p̂)(1/n_A + 1/n_B)) = √(0.062625 × 0.937375 × 0.000050) = 0.001713.
z = 0.0053 / 0.001713 = 3.064; one-sided p = 1 − Φ(3.064) = 0.00109 < 0.05 → reject H₀: B converts better.
  • 1 pt: rates and lift
  • 2 pts: pooled p̂ and SE
  • 2 pts: z
  • 1 pt: p-value
  • 1 pt: decision in words
Q3. Testing against a minimum lift (4 pts)
z = (0.0053 − 0.003) / 0.001713 = 1.313; one-sided p = 0.0945 > 0.05 → fail to reject: the lift isn't shown to be ≥ 0.3 pts.
Both are right: the observed lift (0.525 pts) is clearly above 0 but within about 0.28 pts (one-sided 95%) of 0.3, so it's clearly positive but not clearly ≥ 0.3.
  • 2 pts: z and p
  • 1 pt: decision
  • 1 pt: reconciliation
Q4. Pricing the lift (7 pts)
Extra bookings = 150,000 × 0.0053 = 788/month; × $22 = $17,325/month.
Payback = 25,000 / (17,325/30) = 43.3 days.
Unpooled SE = √(p_A(1−p_A)/n_A + p_B(1−p_B)/n_B) = 0.001713; 95% CI for the lift = 0.0053 ± 1.96 × 0.001713 = [0.00189, 0.00861];
× 150,000 × $22 = [$6,244, $28,406] per month. (Unpooled, because a CI estimates the difference rather than assuming H₀.)
  • 2 pts: monthly dollars
  • 1 pt: payback
  • 2 pts: unpooled SE and lift CI
  • 2 pts: dollar CI
Q5. Sample size (6 pts)
n = (1.645 + 0.842)² × (0.04 × 0.96 + 0.044 × 0.956) / 0.004² = 6.185169 × 0.080464 / 0.000016 = 31,105.2 → 31,106 per arm.
Days = 2 × 31,106 / 5,000 = 12.44 → 13 days.
Halving the detectable lift roughly quadruples n (n ∝ 1/d²; the variances barely change): about 124,424 per arm.
  • 3 pts: n with work shown, rounded up
  • 1 pt: days
  • 2 pts: ~4× with the 1/d² reason
Q6. Peeking (4 pts)
Each look is another chance for noise to cross the threshold, and they stop at the first crossing, so false alarms accumulate: about 19% for 10 looks (exactly 19.3% for equally spaced looks), not 5%. Fixes: fix the sample size and test once at the end; or use a sequential design with alpha spending (e.g. O'Brien–Fleming boundaries).
  • 1 pt: roughly 15–25%
  • 2 pts: multiple looks + stopping at the first crossing
  • 1 pt: a valid fix

Part 2 · Choice models (Q7–Q10)

Q7. Logit shares (4 pts)
e^1.2 = 3.3201, e^0.8 = 2.2255, e^0 = 1; total = 6.5457.
P(A) = 0.507, P(B) = 0.340, P(Not now) = 0.153.
ε is everything about the choice the model can't see (tastes, the trip, mood). Assuming iid Gumbel (type I extreme value) errors gives P_j = e^{V_j}/Σ_k e^{V_k}.

Q8. Reading a logit fit, WTP (7 pts)
(a) z = coef/SE: bag 7.5, free changes 1.5, price −12.5. Bag and price are significant (|z| > 1.96); free changes is not.
(b) WTP = coef / (−price): bag = 0.75/0.025 = $30.00; free changes = 0.30/0.025 = $12.00 (imprecise: its z is only 1.5).
(c) Value $42.00 vs price $35, so it's worth +$7.00 to the average customer. Priced below value, though the flex part is uncertain.
  • 2 pts: z values and significance
  • 3 pts: both WTPs with the minus sign handled
  • 2 pts: comparison and caveat
Q9. IIA (5 pts)
Before: e^0.9/(e^0.9 + 1) = 0.711. After: 2e^0.9/(2e^0.9 + 1) = 0.831.
Really, an identical copy adds nothing new, so buying should stay at about the first number, with Standard's buyers split between two names. The cause is IIA (independence of irrelevant alternatives, from iid errors): the copy takes share in proportion from every option, including Not now. A nested logit (or a mixed logit with correlated errors) groups the twins.
  • 2 pts: both shares
  • 1 pt: realistic outcome
  • 1 pt: IIA named and explained
  • 1 pt: alternative model

Q12. ε-greedy floor (4 pts)
Floor = ε × (average gap over the 3 arms) × T = 0.1 × (0.015 + 0 + 0.025)/3 × 60,000 = 80 upgrades.
It explores 10% of the time forever, whatever it has learned. Thompson sampling (or decaying ε / UCB) explores less as it becomes confident.
  • 2 pts: floor with work
  • 1 pt: why it persists
  • 1 pt: alternative
Q13. ε-greedy probabilities (3 pts)

Q14. Thompson sampling (6 pts)
(a) Beta(1 + opens, 1 + non-opens): A Beta(3, 5), mean 0.375; B Beta(5, 4), mean 0.556; C Beta(2, 2), mean 0.500.
(b) Highest draw: C (0.61).
(c) Not opened adds 1 to the second parameter: Beta(2, 3), mean 0.400.
(d) C's posterior is wide (little data), so it often produces high draws. That's how Thompson explores, and it does less of it as posteriors narrow.
  • 2 pts: posteriors and means
  • 1 pt: highest draw (not highest mean)
  • 2 pts: update
  • 1 pt: exploration explanation

Q17. Overfitting, Ridge and LASSO (5 pts)
(a) Degree 2: lowest validation RMSE (1.12). Degrees 0–1 underfit (both errors high); 3 and up overfit (training keeps falling, validation rises, exploding at 9).
(b) Degree 9 has 10 parameters for 10 points, so it interpolates every point: it fits the noise, which is memorizing, not learning.
(c) Ridge (λΣβ²) shrinks all coefficients smoothly toward zero; LASSO (λΣ|β|) sets some exactly to zero, so it also selects features. Neither penalizes the intercept.
  • 2 pts: degree 2 and the under/overfit ranges
  • 1 pt: interpolation
  • 2 pts: Ridge vs LASSO
Q18. Cutoffs and costs (7 pts)
(a) α = 0.3: accuracy 0.865, precision 0.405, recall 0.750, cost 200×220 + 900×50 = $89,000.
(a) α = 0.6: accuracy 0.927, precision 0.704, recall 0.475, cost 200×40 + 900×105 = $102,500.
(b) α = 0.3: it costs $89,000 vs $102,500, even though α = 0.6 is more accurate (0.927 vs 0.865). Misses cost 4.5× as much as false alarms, so the cheaper rule is the one that flags more customers.
(c) Flag when p × 900 > (1 − p) × 200, i.e. p > 200/(200 + 900) = 0.182.
  • 3 pts: metrics and costs
  • 2 pts: choice by cost (not accuracy)
  • 2 pts: break-even
Q19. ROC and AUC (6 pts)
(a) For each positive, count negatives below it (+½ per tie): 0.9 → 5, 0.7 → 4, 0.6 → 3 + ½, 0.4 → 3; total 15.5 of 4 × 5 = 20 pairs → AUC = 0.775.
(b) A randomly chosen positive scores higher than a randomly chosen negative 77.5% of the time.
(c) Flagged: positives 0.9, 0.7, 0.6 → TPR = 3/4 = 0.75; negatives 0.8, 0.6 → FPR = 2/5 = 0.40.
  • 3 pts: pair counting with ties
  • 1 pt: interpretation
  • 2 pts: TPR and FPR
Q20. Audit a model report (7 pts)
Any three, with fixes:
• Leakage: follow-up visit attended happens after discharge. Drop it or use only what's known at discharge. Also: a random split by stay puts the same patient in train and test. Split by patient or by time.
• Matrix: precision = 300/400 = 0.75, not 0.80; recall = 300/600 = 0.50, unreported. Recompute every metric from the matrix and report recall.
• Metric: accuracy (5,600/6,000 = 0.933) is close to the 0.90 from predicting "no readmission" for everyone. Compare with the baseline and use cost or recall.
• Claim: "cause" from a predictive model. Restate it as association; test with a randomized change if it matters.
